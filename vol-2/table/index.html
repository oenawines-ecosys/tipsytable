Jérôme Bretaudeau

Muscadet “Clos des Bouquinardières”

2023Muscadet, FranceHK$500 a bottle

Tell the table

“Hillside Muscadet from gabbro-rich soils, certified organic since 2009.”

What to taste for Light and dry, with lemon, a salty edge and a long, stony finish.

Why this pairing Lean, salty and lemony, it does what a squeeze of lemon does: lifts the fried tofu puffs and keeps the cold abalone tasting of the sea.

The place This Muscadet is all Melon de Bourgogne, from hillside parcels near Nantes. Their silty, sandy soils are rich in gabbro, an igneous rock.

The maker Jérôme Bretaudeau took over his father’s two hectares in 2001 and now makes wine from eleven grape varieties. He ferments with wild yeasts only and bottles unfined. La Revue du Vin de France named him Winemaker of the Year 2025.

Domaine Cottenceau

Montagny 1er Cru “Les Vignes Longues”

2022Montagny, FranceHK$600 a bottle

Tell the table

“Premier cru Chardonnay from 15-year-old vines, aged 18 to 20 months.”

What to taste for Ripe lemon and white flowers, a touch of toast from the oak, and a firm, clean finish.

Why this pairing A rounder white for richer food. Its ripe lemon cuts the fried shrimp toast, and its weight stands up to the oysters, ginger and scallion.

The place The monks of Cluny favoured the wines of Buxy, today the heart of Montagny. Les Vignes Longues faces east, on clay and limestone.

The maker Maxime Cottenceau’s family grew vines in Buxy for generations and sold the grapes. In 2018 he began bottling from 2.8 hectares of their best plots; he now farms close to four hectares. He worked alongside Vincent Dureuil in Rully.

Domaine Gilbert et Christine Felettig

Fixin 1er Cru “Clos du Chapitre”

2022Fixin, FranceHK$750 a bottle

Tell the table

“Pinot Noir from the cool northern end of the Côte de Nuits.”

What to taste for Slender and firm, with a mineral edge, bright acidity and fresh fruit.

Why this pairing Pinot Noir and roast chicken is a classic: fresh fruit against crisp skin. With the sweet and sour pork, the wine’s acidity keeps pace with the sauce.

The place Clos du Chapitre faces east above 300 metres, on stony soil with little clay. In 1920 Camille Rodier classed it as a grand cru.

The maker In 1965 Henri Felettig began with two hectares of vines and one of blackcurrants; his first own wine came in 1969. His son Gilbert now runs the domaine with his daughter Pauline, from Chambolle-Musigny.

Domaine Rouchier

Saint-Joseph “La Chave”

2021Saint-Joseph, FranceHK$580 a bottle

Tell the table

“Saint-Joseph Syrah from a hectare of granitic gneiss, planted in 1958.”

What to taste for Dark berries, black pepper and a smoky, savoury edge, with firm but fresh tannin.

Why this pairing Syrah’s black pepper and smoke meet the black bean and chilli on the razor clams. The beef and crisp garlic want its tannin.

The place Saint-Joseph runs about 50 km along the Rhône’s right bank, mostly on granite. Its old name, Vin de Mauves, turns up in Les Misérables.

The maker Martine and Christian Rouchier started in 2007 with 1.5 hectares and made 300 bottles that year. Certified organic from the 2013 vintage, they worked the vines with a horse until 2020. Now cover crops and mulch replace tilling.
