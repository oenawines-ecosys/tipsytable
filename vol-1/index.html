Weiser-Künstler

Enkircher Zeppwingert Sekt Brut Nature

2022Mosel, Germany

Tell the table

“Bone-dry sparkling Riesling from steep slate above the Mosel.”

What to taste for Bone-dry, with fine bubbles and no sugar added. Delicate at first, then it lingers. The producer’s own phrase for it: ‘porcelain-doll’ Riesling.

The place Enkirch is a wine village on a loop of the Mosel. Its vines climb steep slopes of dark slate. In the 1800s, Prussian tax officials ranked the Mosel’s vineyards by what their wine earned. Zeppwingert, right on the river, landed in the top class. So the taxman had good taste. Tonight that Riesling comes as Sekt, German for sparkling wine. It is made the Champagne way, with no sugar added at the end.

The maker Konstantin Weiser and Alexandra Künstler started from scratch in 2005, with no family vineyards. They farm a little over 4 hectares of Riesling, by hand. Some of their vines are over 100 years old. When phylloxera wrecked Europe’s vineyards in the late 1800s, most growers replanted on American roots. Their old vines still grow on their own.

Pick a side

Taste the two whites side by side.

The Rhône wine feels rounder in the mouth, with softer acidity. It smells of chamomile tea, chrysanthemum and white flowers, and ends with a salty twist. The Rully has white flowers too, with lemon tart and a light creaminess from the oak. Its acidity, the part that makes your mouth water, is the brighter of the two.

More on Coudoulet Blanc

The place These grapes grow in Orange, just outside Châteauneuf-du-Pape. The Côtes du Rhône name is older than you might think. In 1737 the king ordered casks from the river port of Roquemaure branded C.D.R. It was an early guarantee of where a wine came from. The ground here is covered in round river stones that hold the day’s heat. In Provence, such a stone is a coudoulet, the name on this bottle.

The maker Élodie Jaume’s family has grown grapes around Châteauneuf-du-Pape since 1826. She started with 5 hectares of her own. Then she took over the family estate. It now carries her name. She farms organically and biodynamically. This white comes from 1.3 hectares, nearly two football pitches. That was enough for about 6,500 bottles.

More on Les Plantenays

The place Rully sits at the top of the Côte Chalonnaise, just south of the famous Côte de Beaune. People have prized its wine for centuries. In the early 1800s Rully took up sparkling wine made the Champagne way, and it is still a hub for Crémant de Bourgogne. Tonight’s glass is the still kind.

The maker Mia Subotic took over this family estate from her father in 2020. It bears her first name. The wines are made in underground cellars in Nuits-Saint-Georges, with David Duband. Les Plantenays sits in a hollow. One side of the plot catches the morning sun. The other side gets the evening sun.

Alberto Orte

Escalada do Bibei

2018Galicia, Spain

Tell the table

“A mountain red reviving grapes Valdeorras nearly lost.”

What to taste for Smoky dark fruit, warm baking spices and dried violets, with fine-grained tannin. Think clove and cinnamon, not jam. Similar to Pinot Noir in style: light on its feet, perfumed, more lift than weight.

The place Valdeorras is a mountain valley in eastern Galicia, cut by the River Sil and its tributary, the Bibei. The Romans came here for gold, which they mined from the Sil. Wine here turns up in records from over 1,000 years ago. In the 1880s a vine-killing louse called phylloxera ruined most of the vineyards. Growers replanted with heavy-cropping grapes. Local reds such as Merenzao grew rare. This wine sets out to bring them back.

The maker Alberto Orte co-founded a US importer of Spanish wine at 24, in 1999. Now he makes his own. In Valdeorras he works with a few farmers on tiny plots of 80 to 100-year-old vines. The slopes are too steep for tractors. When a vine dies, he replants with a cutting from the same plot.
